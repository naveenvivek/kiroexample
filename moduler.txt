Yes, it is absolutely possible! In fact, creating reusable AI "blocks" (often called modular microservices or agentic workflows) is exactly where the smartest players in the financial sector are heading right now. It’s highly inefficient for a bank to build a new AI tool from scratch for every single department when they all share the exact same core needs.
Since you asked to tailor this to a "Paris bank," let's look directly at BNP Paribas—headquartered in Paris and one of the largest financial institutions globally. They are actively doing exactly what you are envisioning: moving away from isolated experiments and building foundational, reusable AI models (in partnership with companies like Mistral AI) that can be deployed across hundreds of different use cases with just a little customization.
Here is how your idea of reusable AI blocks maps directly to what is happening in the real world of Paris banking:
1. The Reporting Block
Instead of manually compiling data from different sources, a reusable AI block can be designed to ingest raw data and synthesize it into standardized reports.
 * How it’s customized: The core "block" is the AI's ability to read and summarize. You just customize the instructions: tell it to output a risk compliance report for the legal team, or a portfolio summary for an investor.
 * Paris Bank Reality: BNP Paribas rolled out an internal AI assistant called "Yara Invest." The core AI block is used by private banking teams to synthesize complex investment reports, translate documents, and generate tailored financial insights for clients.
2. The Data Format Conversion Block
Banks run on a mix of ancient legacy systems and modern cloud apps, meaning data format conversion is a constant headache. An AI block can act as a universal translator, taking unstructured data (like a messy email, a PDF, or legacy code) and structuring it into a clean, standardized format.
 * How it’s customized: The block is trained on universal data extraction. You just customize the API to map the extracted data to specific destination fields depending on the department.
 * Paris Bank Reality: At BNP Paribas, their Trade Finance division deals with massive amounts of paperwork (sometimes 100-page printed documents for a single transaction). They built an AI and Optical Character Recognition (OCR) block to automatically identify, capture, and convert that physical data into digital formats, cutting processing time drastically.
3. The Forms & Processing Block
Every bank deals with forms—opening accounts, applying for loans, resolving disputes. A centralized AI block can be built to read a form, validate the information against a database, and flag missing details.
 * How it’s customized: The core block knows how to validate documents. You just customize the rules engine: "For this workflow, check for a salary slip and tax return; for that workflow, check for a government ID."
 * Paris Bank Reality: BNP Paribas is using in-house AI document recognition to automate the verification of mortgage applications. By automating the checking of salary slips and purchase agreements, they are cutting the time to issue a mortgage offer from 10 days down to a targeted 3 days.
The "Build Once, Customize Everywhere" Strategy
To make your vision work, you would set up a centralized "Catalog of Services." Your core engineering team builds the generic AI blocks (the Data Extractor, the Report Generator, the Form Validator).
When the retail banking team wants to automate loan processing, they don't hire AI developers. They just "check out" the Form Validator block from your catalog, customize the prompts to look for loan-specific data, and plug it into their existing software.
The Reality Check
While the technology is completely viable, the main hurdles you will face aren't about the AI itself. The real challenges are:
 * Technical Debt: If you generate too much code or too many customized blocks without strict oversight, it becomes a nightmare to maintain and update.
 * Security & Privacy: Because these blocks process highly sensitive financial data, they cannot be run on public models like standard ChatGPT. They must be hosted locally within the bank's secure infrastructure to ensure client data never leaves the building.
